Key points
- MRLs apply to 315 fresh products and to the same products after processing, adjusted to take account of dilution or concentration during the process.
- Legislation covers pesticides currently or formerly used in agriculture in or outside the EU (around 1100).
- A general default MRL of 0.01 mg/kg applies where a pesticide is not specifically mentioned.
- The safety of all consumer groups is covered e.g. babies, children and vegetarians.
- The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) assesses the safety for consumers based on the toxicity of the pesticide, the maximum levels expected on food and the different diets of Europeans.
- Clear roles of the Member States, EFSA and the Commission in the setting of MRLs.

Regulation amending the Maximum Reside Levels for the neonicotinoid substances clothianidin and thiamethoxam
Insect decline is a global phenomenon caused by multiple factors, one of them is the use of pesticides. Especially for the group of neonicotinoids, risks to bees have been identified. Based on the available knowledge, reducing the use of neonicotinoids is an effective action to tackle pollinators decline. Pollinators play an important role in supporting ecosystem functions and food production worldwide. Food and feed consumed in the EU should not contribute to the global decline of pollinators, independently of whether the product is produced in the EU or imported from non-EU countries. For this reason, a new Regulation was adopted to lower the Maximum Residues Levels (MRLs) for two neonicotinoids (clothianidin and thiamethoxam) to limit of quantification (technical zero).
This Regulation is part of the EU’s ambition to promote sustainable food production on a global stage, which is a component of the Farm to Fork Strategy and it is coordinated with other EU programmes such as the EU pollinators initiative, addressing the main known causes for pollinator decline and strengthening the collaboration between all the actors concerned. In view of the important role that pollinators play in supporting food production, the EU considers that their protection is essential in sustainable food systems.
This is the first time that a Regulation lowering MRLs is based on environmental grounds. It will become applicable in 2026 to ensure that it permits operators in third countries, especially in least developed and developing countries, to adapt to the new rules A corrigendum amending Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/334 corrects a mistake in footnote 19 (page 31, recital 8).
The EU is actively supporting the generation and implementation of more sustainable alternatives to promote the transition towards sustainable production. In this context, it funds several programmes to help non-EU countries, in particular developing countries, to comply with EU legislation. In addition the EU remains actively involved at international cooperation and coordination for a, promoting harmonised measures to tackle environmental issues of global concern as environmental problems that transcend national boundaries cannot be addressed by one country – or region – alone.
