From the supermarket shelf to your kitchen table, packaging plays a vital role in keeping food safe. Food packaging is more than a container — it is an important component of the EU’s food safety system. It helps prevent contamination, maintain hygiene and protect the integrity of food throughout its journey.
How the EU ensures safe food packaging
All packaging materials intended to come into contact with food — such as plastic, paper, glass and metal — must comply with strict EU safety standards. Materials must not transfer substances into food that could endanger health, change the food’s composition or affect its taste or smell.
The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) assesses the safety of substances used in packaging before they are authorised. For certain materials such as plastics, the EU has introduced specific legislation that defines:
- authorised substances – only listed substances can be used in food contact materials
- conditions of use – such as intended food types and temperature limits
- migration limits – maximum levels of permitted substance transfer from packaging into food, ensuring exposure remains within safe levels.
To protect consumers, the EU has banned or restricted several harmful substances, including:
- lead, cadmium, mercury, and bisphenol A (BPA)
- emerging risks such as per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), which are being addressed through new restrictions.
Manufacturers are also required to apply good manufacturing practices to ensure packaging is produced consistently and hygienically.
Full traceability of packaging materials
EU law requires that all food packaging materials be fully traceable throughout their lifecycle — from material production to manufacturing, distribution and sale. This traceability system enables:
- swift responses to any safety issues
- targeted product recalls or withdrawals
- better enforcement and risk management.
When risks are identified, the Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed (RASFF) allows EU countries to act quickly to protect consumers by sharing safety alerts in real time.
