Food allergies and intolerances affect a significant portion of the population, making accurate food labelling essential for consumer safety. The European Union establishes comprehensive requirements for providing food information, including the mandatory declaration of certain allergens.
Which allergens must be declared?
There are 14 allergens that must be clearly indicated if they are used as ingredients in food products sold in the EU:
| Cereals containing gluten wheat, rye, barley, oats, etc. | Crustaceans crabs, prawns, lobsters, etc. | Eggs | Fish |
| Peanuts | Soybeans | Milk | Nuts almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamia/Queensland nuts |
| Celery | Mustard | Sesame seeds | Sulphur dioxide and sulphites when used as preservatives in concentrations above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/L |
| Lupin | Molluscs mussels, oysters, squid, snails, etc. | ||
Where can information on allergens be found?
Allergens must be emphasised within the list of ingredients, for example, by using a different font, style, or background colour, to ensure they are easily identifiable by consumers.
For non-prepacked foods – such as those sold in restaurants, cafés or deli counters – allergen information must be provided. This information can be communicated verbally, but there must be clear indications on how consumers can obtain this information, and it must be available in written form upon request.
‘May contain’ warning
In cases where there is a risk of unintentional presence of allergens due to cross-contamination, businesses may use precautionary allergen labelling, such as "may contain" statements. However, such statements should not be used as a substitute for good manufacturing practices and must be based on a thorough risk assessment.
Who enforces the rules?
Member States are responsible for monitoring compliance with allergen labelling requirements and must establish penalties for non-compliance.

Food allergies and intolerances affect millions of people across the EU. To protect consumers, EU legislation requires clear and consistent labelling of allergens in both prepacked and non-prepacked foods. This allows individuals with food allergies to make safe and informed choices.
